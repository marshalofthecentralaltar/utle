# Heard Light

**The philosophy.** A voice leaves no mark, so the work must build one: a surface that records speech the way an instrument records a pulse. Everything on the canvas is evidence of listening. The composition is a transcript turned into architecture: a few spoken lines, set like specimens, each followed by the quiet fact of what happened. Nothing explains. The viewer reads a conversation between a person and a machine and understands the product from its rhythm alone.

**Space and form.** The ground is near-black, the black of a screen before it wakes, and it is generous: more than half the page is empty by intention. Elements sit on a strict column grid with wide margins, and the lower edge of the page carries one heavy horizontal band, the way a floor carries a room. Forms are rectangles with softened corners and one perfect circle repeated as a dot: the dot is the piece's atom, the light that shows something is being heard. Rows of dots become a waveform, a measurement, a calm pattern that rewards a second look.

**Colour and material.** Ivory on near-black for everything that must be read. Two accents only, each with a meaning and never decorative: a clear green for "listening" and a warm amber for "done". Colour is a signal, not a mood. The material is matte; no gradients, no glows, no shadows beyond a hairline. The result should feel printed by someone who checked every edge, the product of deep expertise and painstaking attention, not of a template.

**Scale and rhythm.** One word is enormous and lowercase; everything else is small and even. Between the two there is almost nothing, so the eye jumps from the name to the lines of speech and then to the band. The spoken lines are set in a monospaced voice, like a log, each on its own measured row; the replies are set smaller and in amber. Rhythm comes from repetition at equal intervals, meticulously aligned, the way a master typesetter keeps a page quiet.

**Composition and hierarchy.** Top left, the name and its light. Centre, the transcript, with air around it. Bottom, the band, which is the product itself drawn at full width: a square, a line of words, a line of what was done. Labels are whispered: tiny, uppercase, spaced, placed where a scientist would put a figure number. Text never exceeds a sentence. Every alignment, every optical margin, every dot spacing is the outcome of countless refinements, so the page reads as an artefact from someone at the absolute top of their field.
